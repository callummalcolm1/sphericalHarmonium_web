The ‘Spherical Harmonium’ is being developed with physicists Derek Glazier and Ken Livingston at the University of Glasgow. The instrument transforms spectrography data of hybrid-meson decompositions into a timeline of audible movement. The data used represents the mass-dependant contributions of spherical harmonic ‘moments’. Each harmonic describes how strongly a particular geometric mode of the decay is present as the system evolves across the mass spectrum.

The data has come from the GlueX experiment - located at Jefferson Lab in Virginia, America. GlueX points a high-intensity beam of polarised photons at a liquid hydrogen target. The resulting collisions produce a short-lived meson particle, the decay is captured by a large cylindrical detector array. The experiment was designed specifically to search for and understand exotic hybrid mesons - particles whose internal gluonic field carries quantum numbers beyond those of ordinary quark-antiquark states.
